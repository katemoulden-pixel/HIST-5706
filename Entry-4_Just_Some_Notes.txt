## Open Science in Archaeology
By way too many people including Shawn Graham

New norms like data stewardship instead of ownership. Transparency over secrecy. Public involvement over exclusion.


5 Themes of open science by Fetcher and Friesike
" 1. infra structure (i.e., creating tools and services to improve research efficiency), 
2. the public (i.e., making science accessible for non scientists), 
3. measurement (i.e., developing alternative metrics to measure the impact of research), 
4. democracy (i.e., making knowledge freely accessible to all),
5. and pragmatics (i.e., making collaborative research more efficient)."
The author's elements of open science:
open access, open data, and open methods.

6. Open Access
	1. Pre-prints and publication costs money
	2. this disadvantages many people
	3. Publications being easily accessible can be helpful for students and nonacademic collaborators such as local and indigenous communitites.
7. Open Data
	1. Open access to datasets
	2. Many people hope to recoup the costs through publication fees and exclusive access "support me on Patreon"
	3. BUT there is potential harm
	4. Think about intellectual property so make sure you negotiate, have legal instruments (CC licenses) or technical solutions (redaction) limiting spacial precision, restricting access, or embargos.
	5. Address negative impacts prior to opening data to the public
	6. The advantages include that citations and increase of impact of work for researchers. Easy to research
8. Open Methods
	1. methods of data collection, analysis and visualisation that are available for inspection and reuse by the public.can include emperical methods -eg. chemicals used to prepare samples, computation and statistical methods (eg. details of taking raw data and producing statistical models, tests and visualisations.)
	2. Important for improving [[reproducibility]] of research (reproduce a study) to get the same results by using the same methods and materials (cornerstone of science)
	3. recommended practices
		1. transaprent software environment like R or PYthon
		2. analyse data instead of softweare 
		3. use version control system
		4. open source lisences
		5. archiving on trustworthy repositories
Why is this relevant?
- transparent reusable and easily accessible without barriers helps educate folks about software that enables [[reproducibility]] 
- promotes archaeological ethics - transparency and reproducibility allow you to build on colleagues work, enhance credibility (more thorough peer review), demonstrate chain of reasoning
- Community best practice facilitate sharing and encourage deposits in trustworthy online resources. Promotes inclusiveness
